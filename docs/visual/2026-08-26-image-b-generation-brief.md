## Purpose

Image B closes the **environment + personal-boat visual language** before the final Representative Visual GDD.

It must prove that:

```text
DAWN | BRIGHT | SUNSET | NIGHT
```

are four atmosphere states of **the same small personal boat on the same sea**, not four separate maps or four unrelated key-art scenes.

Image A customization is already approved and must not be reopened here.

## Shared canon

Preserve:

- `HANDPAINTED_STORYBOOK_3D_DIORAMA`;
- 3/4 small-boat diorama foundation;
- stable calm horizon;
- sea/horizon as primary visual mass;
- matte, low-gloss, broad-value, hand-painted surface language;
- low visual noise;
- cozy lived-in boat rather than inventory showroom;
- mobile-portrait readability even though this comparison board itself may be landscape;
- no combat/cinematic spectacle, no weather threat, no collectible-pressure framing.

## Canvas and comparison layout

Preferred board: wide landscape, approximately 16:9 or 2:1.

Use four equal primary panels in one row or a clean 2×2 grid:

```text
DAWN | BRIGHT | SUNSET | NIGHT
```

Every panel must preserve **exactly the same**:

- camera angle and focal length feeling;
- boat position and scale;
- horizon height;
- deck layout;
- decor positions;
- sea geometry/composition;
- player/pet presence or absence.

Only atmosphere variables may change:

- sky color/value;
- sea color/value/reflection response;
- broad key/fill balance;
- lantern/living-light contribution;
- very restrained cloud/star presence.

If any panel changes framing, map layout, prop placement, or boat identity, the comparison is invalid.

## Representative test boat

Use the existing personal-boat language rather than inventing a new luxury or fantasy vessel.

Preferred test cluster uses **existing approved motifs only** as a provisional comparison set:

- lantern — warm living-light anchor;
- mug — quiet daily-life trace;
- cushion or pet cushion — softness/rest cue;
- small plant — low-noise lived-in accent;
- postcard — memory/personal trace if it does not clutter the deck.

This is a comparison cluster, not automatic final decor canon. The board must make it possible to decide whether 3, 4, or 5 visible items is the right density.

Do not add:

- crates of collectibles;
- treasure/chests;
- rarity glow;
- fishing trophies;
- weapons;
- luxury furniture;
- unrelated decorative clutter;
- new branded props.

## Player and pet treatment

Image B is not a character-selection board.

Preferred:

- either omit player/pet detail entirely;
- or show very small neutral resting silhouettes only to preserve scale/context.

Do not privilege one Image A character family or pet species as the exclusive canon. If one example pair is visible, it is only a scale/context placeholder from the already approved selectable sets.

## Four atmosphere states

### Dawn

Intent: quiet arrival / day beginning without motivational pressure.

- cool desaturated blue/lavender overall field;
- soft peach or warm-gray horizon transition;
- low contrast;
- lantern may still contribute a faint warm pool;
- sea remains readable, not fog-obscured;
- no dramatic sunrise rays.

### Bright

Intent: clear, breathable rest — not harsh noon.

- soft sky blue / pale cyan family;
- slightly clearer sea separation than Dawn;
- gentle broad daylight;
- restrained specular response;
- no blown white highlights;
- no tropical vacation saturation.

### Sunset

Intent: emotional warmth without becoming cinematic or romantic spectacle.

- muted peach / dusty rose / warm amber transition;
- warm light on boat surfaces but sea remains primary;
- slightly stronger lantern relationship may begin;
- no giant sun, lens flare, fiery sky, or orange overload.

### Night

Intent: safe, intimate, quiet continuation of the same place.

- deep but desaturated navy/blue-green family;
- horizon still readable;
- lantern becomes the clear warm living accent;
- sparse, low-contrast stars if used;
- no pitch-black sea;
- no giant moon, supernatural glow, or high-contrast rim lighting.

## Value hierarchy

Every panel should read in this order:

```text
sea / horizon
→ small personal boat as one coherent place
→ warm lived-in decor trace
→ optional tiny player/pet context
```

Never:

```text
lantern glow / character face
→ decor collection
→ boat
→ sea as background
```

## Questions Image B must let the user answer

1. Do all four panels unmistakably feel like the same boat and same place?
2. Which time state best represents the project in the final Representative Visual GDD?
3. Which state feels most restful for long viewing?
4. Does Night remain readable and safe rather than dark/dramatic?
5. Does Sunset stay warm without becoming cinematic?
6. Is the representative decor cluster better at 3, 4, or 5 visible motifs?
7. Is lantern light the right single warm living accent?
8. Does sea/horizon remain more important than decor in every state?

## Rejection / regeneration triggers

Reject or regenerate if:

- different panels use different camera/boat/decor layouts;
- time states look like different maps;
- Night hides the sea/horizon;
- Sunset becomes orange spectacle;
- Bright becomes glossy/tropical/overexposed;
- Dawn becomes foggy enough to erase the space;
- lantern or decor becomes the focal point;
- character/pet detail reopens Image A decisions;
- the boat looks expensive, fantastical, photoreal, or materially unrelated to Proof 01/02;
- generated UI/text is treated as meaningful design content.
